The idea in three steps

Reusing medicine we already trust

Inventing a new drug takes a decade and a billion dollars. Repurposing asks a faster question: could a medicine that is already approved and known to be safe also work against a neglected disease? Here is how GeneTropica looks for the answer.

1

Start with safe drugs

Begin with 100 medicines that are already FDA-approved, so their safety in humans is established. No need to start from scratch.

FDA-approved library
2

Simulate the grip

For each drug, simulate how tightly it latches onto a key protein the pathogen needs to survive. A tighter, well-fitted grip means more potential to disrupt the disease.

AutoDock Vina · 50 ns MD
3

Rank the realistic ones

Score every candidate two ways to avoid bias, keep the drug-like, safe ones, and surface the most promising classes for a scientist to follow up.

Dual-metric ranking

Six doors into three diseases

The proteins we aim at

A pathogen depends on a handful of proteins to enter cells, copy itself and mature. Block one and you can stop the disease. These are the six we screen against.

Dengue

3 targets · ~390M infections / year
PDB 5CCV · headline

NS5 polymerase

The copy machine the virus uses to multiply. Jam it and the virus can't replicate.

PDB 2VBC

NS3 protease-helicase

Molecular scissors and an unzipper the virus needs to mature its proteins.

PDB 1OAN

Envelope (E) protein

The key the virus turns to enter a human cell in the first place.

What this is, and isn't

Impressive only if it's honest.

A prior, not a cure

These are computational predictions that point a scientist toward promising candidates. They are a starting hypothesis, never a treatment.

Sofosbuvir is a control

The known-active hepatitis C drug is included on purpose to check the method, not presented as a discovery. It ranks mid-pack, as expected.

Two metrics on purpose

Raw binding favours big molecules. Efficiency favours tiny ones. Reporting both, over drug-like candidates, avoids fooling ourselves with size bias.

Caveats stay visible

Where validation was weak (the NS5 ROC AUC of 0.37), it's stated plainly rather than hidden. The ML score is a target-agnostic prior, not a per-target oracle.
