# NS5 re-dock at the corrected catalytic site

Generated by `scripts/redock_ns5_corrected.py`. Nothing here is published — the
live site still serves the old poses in `web/public/data/`.

## What was wrong

The NS5 poses on the site were docked into a box centred at (-118.9, 60.8, 40.2)
on 5CCV. That structure's GDD catalytic motif (residues 662-664) is 30.0 A from
that centre, while the 25 A box reaches 12.5 A along an axis and 21.7 A to its
farthest corner. The active site was never inside the search space, so the NS5
ranking and the Escape durability page describe a region that is not the target.

## What was re-docked

    receptor        colab/4V0R_chainA_Mg.pdbqt, chain D
    box centre      (-11.3, 19.2, -7.8)   GDD centroid with the catalytic Mg2+
    box size        25 A, exhaustiveness 8, seed 42
    library         63 drugs, 250-600 Da, 0 failures

Result: **58 of 63 poses (92%) contact the GDD motif**, against 0 of 44 before.
Mean score improved 0.86 kcal/mol. Sofosbuvir, the known-active positive
control, moved from rank 17 to rank 5 (-6.26 -> -8.09 kcal/mol).

## The scores still do not rank

Mean rank of the 7 RdRp inhibitors went 29.1 -> 26.6 of 63; the negative
controls stayed at 31.2. Random is ~32. Sofosbuvir improving is one drug, not
class separation, and this matches the retrospective benchmark's AUC 0.494
(95% CI 0.306-0.684). Correcting the box fixed **where** a drug is placed, not
whether the score can rank drugs.

## Numbering: do not use a fixed offset

`4V0R_to_DENV2_map.json` maps chain D residues to the DENV-2 frame that the
ConSurf grades use, by Biopython global alignment (BLOSUM62, open -11, extend
-1, free end gaps). All five published catalytic residues land correctly
(533, 663, 664, 737, 794) and the GDD reads GDD.

The offset is **not uniform** — residues 1-164 are +469, 169-171 are +465, and
172-415 are +468. Assuming a flat +468 would mis-assign 167 of 411 residues
(41%), including 16 of the 48 residues the poses actually contact. Residue 138
is touched by 55 of 63 poses and would have been labelled 607 instead of 608.
A spot-check near the GDD would not have caught this, because that region falls
in the block where +468 happens to be correct.

## The structure is DENV-3, and that is fine

4V0R chain D is 96.9% identical to DENV-3, versus 80.2% to DENV-2. The
conservation grades are DENV-2's. Of the 48 contacted residues, only 3 genuinely
differ between the serotypes:

| DENV-2 pos | DENV-3 | DENV-2 | grade | poses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 698 | K | R | 4 variable | 3 |
| 695 | Q | E | 4 variable | 2 |
| 730 | P | N | 3 variable | 1 |

All three are already graded variable, so the grades and the serotype difference
agree. Scoring DENV-3 poses against DENV-2 grades is sound; the page should
state that the structure is DENV-3.

## RESOLVED: residue 795 barely matters

Rebuilding the leaderboard both ways (`scripts/rebuild_escape_corrected.py`)
settles this empirically: excluding residue 795 changes the durability of
**3 drugs of 63, by at most 1 point**. The earlier "28 of 63 poses contact it"
figure came from a crude 4.5 A any-atom sweep; the chemistry-aware classifier
the pipeline actually uses is stricter, and a backbone-plus-CB stub offers
little for it to score. No exclusion is needed. The caveat below is still worth
stating on any page that publishes these numbers, but it does not change them.

## The original concern, for the record: residue 795 is a truncated side chain

DENV-2 795 (chain D residue 327) is a **tryptophan whose side chain was not
resolved in the crystal**. It is modelled as a backbone-plus-CB stub labelled
ALA — 6 atoms where a tryptophan has 14. Six other unresolved side chains in
chain D are truncated the same way (real residues Q, R, R, S, W, W).

This one matters because **28 of 63 poses (44%) contact it**, and it carries
conservation grade 8 (conserved), so it adds to durability scores. The structure
has a cavity where a bulky side chain actually sits, and poses may be occupying
space the real protein fills.

Before rebuilding `escape.json`, either repair that side chain and re-dock, or
exclude 795 from durability scoring and say so.

## THE LEADERBOARD DOES NOT SURVIVE THE CORRECTION

This is the main result of the rebuild, and it argues against republishing the
Escape page with corrected numbers.

Durability is the mean ConSurf grade of a drug's contact residues, rescaled to
0-100. Corrected poses all land in the catalytic site -- which is, by
definition, the most conserved part of the protein. **34 of the 42 residues the
poses touch grade 7 or higher.** So every drug grips conserved residues, and
every drug scores high:

| | live (wrong-site poses) | corrected (catalytic site) |
|---|---|---|
| n | 44 | 63 |
| range | 59-79% | 77-96% |
| sd | 5.4 | 4.2 |
| 85-94% band | -- | 46 of 63 drugs |

**The median durability gap between adjacent drugs is 0.0 points**, and ten
drugs share the single score 90%. Most of the ordering is an arbitrary
tie-break. Dropping any one contact moves a drug by 1-2 points, which is more
than the gap separating most neighbours -- and the poses themselves are not
reproducible (see below), so which contacts a drug has is itself unstable.

The old leaderboard's wider apparent spread was an artifact of the error: a box
that missed the active site scattered poses across regions of varying
conservation, manufacturing differences between drugs. Correcting the box
removes that spread. The ranking looked more informative when it was wrong.

Celecoxib, the drug the live page crowns most durable, falls from rank 1 to
rank 14. Doxycycline rises from 24 to 1. Neither ordering is meaningful.

Conclusion: the corrected data is suitable for saying *which residues these
drugs contact* and *that the catalytic site is highly conserved*. It does not
support ranking drugs by durability. Both rebuilt files are kept in
data/docking_corrected/ as evidence, and neither should be published as a
leaderboard.

## Runs are not bit-for-bit reproducible

`--seed 42` fixes Vina's search but not the input geometry.
`prepare_ligands.smiles_to_3d` embeds the starting conformer with an unseeded
`ETKDGv3()`; the seeded call on the next line is only a fallback for embedding
failures. Repeat runs vary by about 0.2 kcal/mol -- celecoxib gave -6.95 and
-6.79 on two runs. The top and bottom of the table are stable, but mid-table
ranks are not stable to that spread, so rank changes of a few places should not
be read as meaningful.

The pose files are not committed (`.gitignore:20` ignores `*.pdbqt` repo-wide),
so `results.json` and this file are the durable record. Threading an optional
seed through `smiles_to_3d` would make the run reproducible and let the poses
be regenerated exactly.

## File hygiene

`colab/4V0R_chainA_Mg.pdbqt` is misnamed: it holds four protein chains (A, B, C,
D) plus a stray UNL ligand, not chain A alone. Chain D is the polymerase. This
does **not** corrupt the docking — 363 of the 364 receptor atoms inside the box
belong to chain D and the UNL sits 45 A away — but any analysis keyed on residue
number alone must filter to chain D first. The same file drove the AUC 0.494
benchmark.
